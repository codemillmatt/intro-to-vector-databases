# The Last Lighthouse Keeper

## Chapter 1: Sixty Years of Light

Thomas McCallister stood at the top of the Widow's Point Lighthouse, as he had every evening for the past sixty years. His weathered hands rested on the brass rail, worn smooth by generations of keepers before him.

Tomorrow, they would come to take him away. Not unkindly—the coast guard had been apologetic when they delivered the news. Automation had finally reached even this remote stretch of the Maine coastline. The light would continue to burn, but it would no longer need a keeper.

"Sixty years," Thomas murmured to the endless grey sea. He had been twenty-two when he first climbed these stairs, a young man fleeing a broken heart and seeking solitude. He had found both in abundance.

The lighthouse had become his wife, the sea his confidante, and the ships that passed in the night his only companions. Some might call it a lonely life. Thomas called it peace.

## Chapter 2: The Storm of '62

Thomas settled into his worn armchair, letting his mind drift back through the decades. So many memories lived in these circular walls.

He thought of the Great Storm of 1962, when the waves had risen so high they crashed against the lantern room itself. He had kept the light burning through three days of fury, manually rotating the lens when the motor failed, knowing that ships were out there, depending on him.

When the storm finally broke, he learned that three vessels had made it safely to harbor by following his light. The captains had come to thank him in person, weathered men with tears in their eyes.

"You saved our lives," Captain Morrison had said, gripping Thomas's hand.

"I just kept the light burning," Thomas had replied. "The light saved you."

But he had allowed himself a small measure of pride. That was what lighthouse keepers did—they held back the darkness.

## Chapter 3: The Girl in the Red Coat

The sea had given Thomas many things over the years, but also taken. He remembered the winter of 1978, when he had spotted something red tumbling in the surf.

It was a child's coat. His heart had seized as he scrambled down the rocky shore, certain he would find a body. Instead, he found a little girl, perhaps four years old, clinging to a piece of wooden debris.

He never learned her full story—how she had come to be in the water, or what had happened to her family. The authorities had eventually placed her with relatives in Boston.

But for three weeks, she had lived with him in the lighthouse. He had taught her to feed the seabirds and to read the weather in the clouds. She had called him "Grandpa Light."

Years later, a woman came to visit him. She was grown now, with children of her own, but she still wore a red coat.

"I never forgot you," she said. "I never forgot how you saved me and kept me warm."

They still exchanged letters every Christmas.

## Chapter 4: The Final Night

Thomas made his final rounds as the sun set over Widow's Point. He polished the brass fixtures, checked the diesel levels, and wound the backup clockwork mechanism that he alone still knew how to maintain.

As darkness fell, the light came on automatically now—it had for decades. But Thomas stood beside it anyway, watching the beam sweep across the water.

A ship passed in the distance, its lights twinkling against the darkness. Thomas raised his hand in a silent salute, as he had to thousands of ships over the years.

"Keep safe," he whispered. "There's always a light to guide you home."

In the morning, he would pack his few belongings and leave this place that had been his entire world. The coast guard had arranged for him to live in a small cottage in town, where well-meaning people would check on him and make sure he ate properly.

It wouldn't be the same. Nothing ever would be.

But standing there in the glow of the light, Thomas McCallister realized he had no regrets. He had devoted his life to a simple, noble purpose: keeping the light burning so others could find their way home.

And in the end, what more could any person ask for?

The beam swept out over the dark water, as it would for years to come. Thomas smiled, placed his hand on the warm glass of the lantern, and whispered his goodnight.

"Keep burning, old friend. Keep burning."
