# The Herbalist’s Daughter

## Chapter 1: The Gatherer

Mist clung to the hills above the village of Kilfenora as Maeve O’Connell moved quietly along the hedgerows. Dawn had not yet broken, and that was exactly how she preferred it. Before the church bell rang, before questions were asked, before fear took hold.

She knelt beside a patch of foxglove, careful not to touch its bell-shaped flowers with bare skin. Her mother had taught her well—what healed in the right measure could kill in the wrong one. Maeve wrapped the roots in linen and tucked them into her satchel alongside yarrow, comfrey, and dried angelica.

Below her, smoke curled from chimneys, thinner than usual. Too many hearths had gone cold.

The sickness had come quietly at first. A cough here, a fever there. Then whole families took to their beds. The priest called it God’s trial. The English magistrate, newly arrived, whispered darker things.

Maeve knew only this: people were dying, and she could not stand aside.

## Chapter 2: Remedies and Rumors

Her father’s cottage smelled of crushed leaves and boiled water. Maeve worked methodically, grinding herbs with a stone pestle as her mother once had.

“You should stop,” her father said softly from his chair by the fire. His leg had never healed properly after the harvest accident. “They’re talking.”

“Let them,” Maeve replied. “If Brigid’s boy survives the night, talk won’t matter.”

By evening, word had spread that the herbalist’s daughter was at work again. Some came in secret, knocking after dark. Others crossed themselves when they passed the cottage.

When Brigid’s boy did survive, rumors hardened into something sharper.

“She whispers to plants.”
“She keeps old ways.”
“She knows things a good woman shouldn’t.”

Maeve felt the weight of their fear more than their suspicion. Fear, she knew, made people dangerous.

## Chapter 3: The Hunters

They arrived on horseback, cloaks dark against the green hills. Two men, boots polished, eyes cold with certainty. Witch hunters, though they called themselves servants of order.

Maeve saw them from the doorway, her hands still stained green.

“We’re looking for a girl,” the taller one said. “A healer.”

“I make teas,” Maeve answered evenly. “Nothing more.”

The man’s gaze flicked to the drying herbs overhead. “That remains to be seen.”

That night, Maeve packed only what she could carry. Her father pressed her mother’s old journal into her hands, its pages filled with careful notes and pressed leaves.

“Go to the coast,” he said. “Follow the old paths.”

Maeve hesitated, then embraced him. She left before dawn, the village silent behind her.

## Chapter 4: What Remains

Weeks later, the sickness broke. Some said it was prayer. Others claimed the hunters had driven the evil away.

A few knew better.

In cottages along the hills, small bundles of herbs appeared at doorsteps. Fevers eased. Coughs softened. No one saw who left them.

Maeve never returned to Kilfenora, but her work did. The old ways survived, passed quietly from hand to hand, mother to daughter.

Years later, a young girl would ask where the knowledge came from.

And someone would answer, simply, “From a woman who refused to let fear decide who lived.”
